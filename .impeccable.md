## Design Context

### Users
Complete beginners (students) at an in-person coding workshop. They sit in a room with a projector, often in groups. Volunteers present the slides while students follow along in VS Code. Their state of mind: slightly anxious, curious, wanting to feel capable. They need clarity above everything else — they can't re-read a slide they missed.

### Brand Personality
Grounded, musical, clear. Not a tech company, not a school, not a music streaming app — closer to a well-designed programming book that happens to love music. Confident and approachable. Treats beginners as intelligent adults.

Three words: **curious · structured · warm**

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light. The workshop runs in daylight or lit rooms. Code should read clearly on projector.
- **Music tie-in**: Moderate. The palette nods to vinyl and warm stage lighting (amber/gold) without becoming a "music app". No music emoji used decoratively, no headphone icons, no waveform illustrations.
- **NOT**: Toy/childish (bubbly fonts, bright primaries). NOT generic corporate (blue gradients, stock photo headers, bullet hell). NOT hacker chalkboard (neon on dark).
- **Reference feel**: A well-typeset technical book with a warm personality. Think: the inside of a Kindle Paperwhite displaying a programming tutorial, or an independent publisher's workshop zine.

### Color Palette
- Background: oklch(0.985 0.008 80) — warm paper white, not sterile
- Text: oklch(0.15 0.010 250) — deep charcoal, slightly cool
- Text muted: oklch(0.42 0.015 250)
- Accent amber: oklch(0.65 0.155 55) — warm gold, evokes vinyl record labels and stage lighting
- Accent light: oklch(0.93 0.04 78) — background tints, code surfaces
- Border: oklch(0.87 0.016 80)
- Section break: oklch(0.20 0.035 260) — deep navy for chapter dividers

### Typography
- **Display/Headings**: Bricolage Grotesque — expressive grotesque with personality, not bubbly
- **Body**: Figtree — clean, friendly, highly legible at presentation sizes
- **Mono**: Fira Code — for code blocks

### Design Principles
1. **Code is the hero** — every design decision should make the code examples easier to read and remember
2. **Chapter breaks should feel like a breath** — section slides pause the flow, signal new territory
3. **Warmth through palette, not decoration** — avoid music clipart; let the amber accent and warm paper do the work
4. **Hierarchy through contrast** — use size and weight aggressively; beginners can't parse subtle hierarchy
5. **Earn every element** — if a line, icon, or border doesn't help comprehension, remove it
